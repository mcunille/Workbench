# H1 narrated walkthrough

Narration: Microsoft Zira (synthetic voice).

## 00:00:00 — H1 · Your first collection

Welcome to Workbench. This is H one: a durable collection notebook. Start with a private, empty collection, then record the individual pieces you want to remember.

## 00:00:13 — Remember a gemstone

Let's add a blue sapphire. Give the piece a recognizable name, describe what matters to you, and record where you keep it. Notes and storage location are optional. Save item opens the complete saved record, including its permanent identifier.

## 00:00:32 — Find your way back

Return to the collection and open the sapphire again. Refreshing keeps its notes and tray location intact. The walkthrough also checks a separate authenticated session against the same database, so this is real persistence, not a temporary browser mockup.

## 00:00:50 — Keep an unfinished thought

If you start another piece and navigate away, Workbench asks before discarding your draft. Keep editing preserves your text. When you deliberately choose Discard changes, the draft is removed without creating an item.

## 00:01:06 — Recover an uncertain save

Now add a silver pendant. For this demonstration, Playwright deliberately drops the response after the server saves it. Workbench explains the uncertain result and offers Retry save. Retrying resolves the original request, and the collection contains just one pendant.

## 00:01:26 — Choose a comfortable appearance

Choose Dark or Light to suit your workspace. System follows your device preference. Your appearance choice is remembered without changing your saved collection or interrupting the task.

## 00:01:39 — Take the notebook to the workbench

On a narrow phone screen, the same collection and item details remain accessible. Open the sapphire to read your notes and find its storage location while you are at the bench.

## 00:01:52 — A clear foundation

H one records individual objects. A gemstone, a jewelry piece, a material sample, or a single consumable container can each have a name, notes, and location. Category specific properties, quantities, consumption, photos, search, and editing are future workflows. Today, you can save a piece, browse your collection, and return to what you recorded.
